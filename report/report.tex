\section{Question and answer}
After an election without a majority, parties bargain over who governs and how the ministries are split.
Real coalition cabinets divide portfolios almost exactly in proportion to seats (\emph{Gamson's law}~\cite{gamson}): on
\RealN{} real cabinets, share $=\RealA+\RealB\,s+\RealG\,F$ ($s$: seat share in the coalition, $F$: prime
minister's party; $R^2$ \RealRtwo). The leading bargaining theory, Baron--Ferejohn (BF)~\cite{bf}, says the
\emph{formateur} who proposes the cabinet should take a large premium. \textbf{We ask which of the two
self-interested parties produce}: we model parties as reinforcement-learning agents rewarded only by their
own office and policy payoffs (no fairness term), check them against exact equilibria, vary the
institutions, and confront the result with real cabinets. \textbf{Answer.} Learning parties favour the
formateur whenever the rules give it proposal power, and split by \emph{voting weight} when parties
bargain by demands; they never produce seat-proportionality as such. Real cabinets do: no premium relative
to seats, and at most a small one (\RobWeightsG) relative to voting weights, far below BF.
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{F\arabic*}]
\item \textbf{Proposal bargaining favours the formateur} as BF predicts (learner validated against the exact
solution), and private values or policy motives only move the premium from the \emph{number} of
ministries to their value or to policy control.
\item \textbf{Demand bargaining removes the premium but follows voting weights, not seats}, exactly as the
equilibrium of our protocol, which we solve (pre-registered tests).
\item \textbf{Real cabinets follow seats}: no detectable premium in counts or key ministries; Gamson's law
predicts real splits better than every learned policy.
\end{enumerate}

\section{RL formulation}
\textbf{Environment} (\code{cabinet/env.py}, \code{cabinet/config.py}): a partially observable stochastic
game with $n$ parties (seats $w_i$, majority quota) and $m$ ministries. In a period without a cabinet a
formateur $f\sim\rho$ proposes a winning coalition $C\ni f$ and an allocation $\mu:\{1..m\}\to C$; the
cabinet forms if every partner accepts and pays per-period flows until the term ends or a member exits.
Switchable mechanisms: repeated terms (M1), demand protocol (M2: a recognised first mover, then the others
in random order, each demands a share or \emph{closes} a cabinet with the cheapest earlier demands and takes
the rest), private types (M4), heterogeneous values (M5), exits (M8), ministerial policy (M9), recognition
rule (M10). Level E0 is the exact BF game; E* has every mechanism on.

\textbf{State $\mathcal S$.} A \emph{set} of $n$ party tokens (26 features: seats, recognition
probability, roles, offers, last $\Lambda$ cabinets), $m$ ministry tokens (8: salience, own valuation,
policy dimension led), pair features, context and decision features; no labels, so one permutation-equivariant
network serves any $n,m$.
\textbf{Action $\mathcal A$} (masked): the formateur includes/excludes each party (masks keep the coalition
winning) and points each ministry to a member; voters accept/reject; members stay/exit; under M2 a party
demands 0--100\% or closes.
\textbf{Reward $\mathcal R$.} $r_i=\sum_{k:\mu(k)=i}\nu_{ik}-\lambda\frac1d\sum_h|y_h-z_{ih}|$ per governed
period (value of own ministries minus policy loss; the holder of a lead ministry sets $y_h$), an exit
bonus on leaving, 0 otherwise; discount $\delta$. Rewards are individual.

\textbf{Algorithm} (\code{cabinet/policy.py}, \code{cabinet/ppo.py}): one shared attention network with
masked autoregressive heads, trained by PPO~\cite{ppo} with GAE on each agent's own decision sequence (semi-MDP);
also MAPPO~\cite{mappo}, fictitious self-play, PSRO (\code{cabinet/psro.py}) and a GRU memory. \textbf{Baselines}: exact BF
(\code{cabinet/bf\_solver.py}), exact demand equilibrium (\code{cabinet/demand\_solver.py}), Gamson, myopic
and random agents (\code{cabinet/baselines.py}). \textbf{Metric} (\code{cabinet/metrics.py}): regression (1)
$p=\alpha+\beta s+\gamma F$ on the cabinets formed, $\gamma$ being the seat-controlled formateur premium,
and its analogues for value and policy-ministry shares. 500--2000 PPO iterations per run, 3--10 seeds
(the statistical units); hyper-parameters tuned on E0 only.

\section{Results}
\begin{figure}[t]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{../figures/fig_e0_benchmark.pdf}

\textbf{F1, proposal bargaining.} \emph{Learner check.} In the symmetric BF game learned shares follow the
closed form (Fig.~\ref{fig:e0}; mean absolute gap \EzeroAbsGap, \EzeroCellsOK/9 cells within 0.02, mean
signed gap \EzeroGap), including its decrease with patience $\delta$ for $n\le5$. This matches equilibrium
\emph{outcomes}, not equilibrium \emph{play}: learned voters would accept far less than their reservation
value $\delta/n$, and against these frozen voters a formateur best response would gain up to \BrGainSeven{}
per formation at $n=7$ (\BrGainThree{} at $n=3$; \code{scripts/bf\_best\_response.py}). Formateurs never
try low offers, so voters never learn to reject them, a self-confirming pattern~\cite{fl} that leaves
proposers with less than theory at $n=7$, as in laboratory BF experiments~\cite{fkm}.
\emph{Institutions.} The base game keeps a premium ($\hat\gamma=\BaseGamma$; \LongBaseG{} after 4$\times$
training). Repetition, even with patient agents and memory, exits and the recognition rule leave it in
place (Fig.~\ref{fig:mech}), as do MAPPO, fictitious self-play and PSRO. Two mechanisms remove the
\emph{counted} premium, private types ($\MfourGamma$) and ministerial policy ($\MnineGamma$), and removing
private types from E* restores it. But the premium moves: in each party's share of the value it places on
ministries the formateur coefficient is \MfourGnu{} with private types and \EstarGnu{} in E*, and with
policy it holds the policy-setting ministries (coefficient \MnineGlead). Both persist with 4$\times$ training.

\textbf{F2, demand bargaining and voting weights.} On the same random 3--8-party parliaments
(\NprotSeeds{} seeds per protocol, tests fixed in advance in \code{docs/PREREGISTRATION.md}), demands
instead of proposals lower the premium by \HoneDiff{} (95\% CI \HoneLo--\HoneHi; exact permutation
$p=\HoneP$) and raise the seat slope by \HtwoDiff{} (\HtwoLo--\HtwoHi); the demand premium is within
$\pm0.05$ (\HthreeMean, 90\% CI \HthreeLo{} to \HthreeHi). Is this Gamson's law? Our protocol is not
Morelli's~\cite{morelli,mvp}, so we solved it exactly (backward induction over all demand histories):
any two of 45/35/20 seats split \textbf{50/50}, because every pair wins, and in 40/30/25/5 the 5-seat party
gets nothing; Gamson predicts 0.56/0.44 for 45+35. Learned demand agents reproduce the equilibrium
(Table~\ref{tab:fixed}): every pair splits within \AfDemMaxDev{} of 50/50 (seat slope \AfDemSlope) and the
dummy is never included. Learned splits follow voting weights; the seat slope of 0.8 in random parliaments
arises because seats and voting weights are correlated there. With default exploration the closer is
under-paid (exploration lock-in; a single \emph{close} action against 101 demand bins), which more
exploration of demands corrects.

\textbf{F3, real cabinets} (Seki--Williams~\cite{sw} linked to ParlGov~\cite{parlgov};
\code{scripts/seki\_eval.py}, \code{scripts/real\_robustness.py}). Relative to seats the prime minister's
party has no premium (\RobBaseG, wild-cluster 95\% CI \RobBaseLo{} to \RobBaseHi; leave-one-country-out
\LocoLo{} to \LocoHi). Relative to minimal integer voting weights it has one, \RobWeightsG{}
(\RobWeightsLo{} to \RobWeightsHi), as Ansolabehere et al.\ report~\cite{asw}; with a largest-party control
\RobLargestG{} (\RobLargestLo{} to \RobLargestHi; identified by \RobPmNotLargest{} cabinets). Seats fit better
than weights ($R^2$ \RobBaseRtwo{} vs \RobWeightsRtwo) and absorb the weight effect when both enter. Key
ministries (finance, foreign, interior, defence, justice; \KeyN{} cabinets) show no detectable premium either, in line with salience-weighted evidence~\cite{wd}:
\KeyShare{} held vs \KeyCount{} of all ministers (difference \KeyDiff, 95\% CI \KeyDiffLo{} to \KeyDiffHi).
Given the real coalition and prime minister, Gamson's law errs by \MaeGamson{} per party, BF by \MaeBF{}
and agents with private types by \MaePrivLo--\MaePrivHi{}, every one worse than Gamson
(Fig.~\ref{fig:real}). Left to bargain freely in the real parliaments (Table~\ref{tab:own}), no protocol
matches everything: demand agents match the seat slope, E* agents the size of the premium on both
scales, proposal agents overshoot it.

\textbf{Reliability.} Best responses trained by PPO (400 iterations) gain \GainLong{} per period against E*
(\GainLongRel{} of a seat's payoff), \GainLongGamson{} and \GainLongMyopic{} against scripted Gamson and
BF-heuristic profiles; cross-play between independent runs costs \CmSame. Because the analytic BF test above
finds gains PPO best responses can miss, these are lower bounds.

\section{Implications and limitations}
To the question of the project: self-interested learning parties do not allocate in proportion to seats.
They favour the formateur exactly as far as the rules give it proposal power, and otherwise split by voting
weight; information and policy motives change the currency of the premium, not its existence. Real cabinets
are more seat-proportional than any protocol we trained, with at most a small premium relative to voting
weights. Gamson's law therefore looks less like the outcome of the bargaining modelled by BF or Morelli
than of rules outside it, such as coalition commitments made before the split~\cite{ks} or within-party
bargaining~\cite{cox}. \textbf{Limitations}: scaled-down sizes; learned profiles are approximate, not exact,
equilibria; the PM's party proxies the formateur; real shares count ministers (salience only through key
ministries); a flat demand head under-explores closing. \textbf{Not done}, with reason: party-specific
manifesto salience (needs registered data access and portfolio coding), a two-stage close/demand head
(retraining every demand run), more seeds (every pre-registered effect is decisive at 10). Every claim and
its source file: \code{docs/AUDIT.md}; specification coverage: \code{docs/SPEC\_COVERAGE.md}.
